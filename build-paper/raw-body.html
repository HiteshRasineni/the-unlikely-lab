@@TOK:HEADID:paper-abstract@@Abstract

We report a search for dark matter (DM) produced in association with a leptonically decaying ZZ boson at s=13\sqrt{s}=13 TeV, using CMS Run 2015D open data corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 2.32fb−12.32~\mathrm{fb}^{-1} and simplified-model Monte Carlo simulation. Events are selected in the mono-Z→ℓ+ℓ−Z\to\ell^+\ell^- final state in two parallel lepton-flavor channels, Z→μ+μ−Z\to\mu^+\mu^- and Z→e+e−Z\to e^+e^-, requiring an opposite-sign same-flavor dilepton pair with invariant mass 60<mℓℓ<120GeV60 < m_{\ell\ell} < 120\,\text{GeV}. A signal region is defined by ETmiss≥50GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}\ge 50\,\text{GeV}, |Δϕ(ETmiss,Z)|>2.5|\Delta\phi(\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}, Z)| > 2.5, and njets≤1n_{\mathrm{jets}} \le 1; a control region with ETmiss<50GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}< 50\,\text{GeV} provides the SM density model used in the likelihood-ratio score. Forty kinematic observables are extracted from MINIAOD and MINIAODSIM inputs, cleaned with physics-motivated bounds and data-driven tail clipping, and reduced to a 37-dimensional feature vector comprising 35 physics quantities augmented with two binary jet-presence indicators. Five Neural Spline Flows (NSFs)@@TOK:CITE:Durkan2019,Papamakarios2021@@ are trained independently: two channel-specific SM flows learn the background density from control-region Drell–Yan events (70%/30% train/validation split), and three mediator-specific DM flows learn the signal density from Monte Carlo samples with vector, axial-vector, and scalar ss-channel mediators. For each mediator hypothesis hh, the per-event test statistic is the log-likelihood ratio 𝒮h(𝐱)=log⁡p(𝐱∣DMh)−log⁡p(𝐱∣SMℓℓ),\mathcal{S}_h(\mathbf{x}) = \log p(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathrm{DM}_h) - \log p(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathrm{SM}_{\ell\ell}), which concentrates signal sensitivity across the full kinematic phase space without requiring a hard upper ETmiss\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}} threshold. The fitted signal strengths are nonzero in the nominal fits, driven by a high-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} background-modelling residual rather than evidence for a DM signal. Combining the μμ\mu\mu and eeee channels in a simultaneous SR+VR binned profile-likelihood fit, where the validation region (50≤ETmiss<100GeV50\le\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}<100\,\text{GeV}) provides an independent background normalisation constraint, we set observed (expected) 95% CL upper limits on the signal-strength parameter of μ<0.0177\mu<0.0177 (0.00180.0018) for the scalar mediator, μ<0.0362\mu<0.0362 (0.00390.0039) for the vector mediator, and μ<0.0498\mu<0.0498 (0.00690.0069) for the axial-vector mediator, corresponding to cross-section upper limits of σ95<1.76×10−9pb\sigma_{95}<1.76\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{pb} (scalar), σ95<9.90×10−4pb\sigma_{95}<9.90\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{pb} (vector), and σ95<9.24×10−2pb\sigma_{95}<9.24\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{pb} and 7.89×10−3pb7.89\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{pb} (axial-vector, two benchmark points). The observed limits exceed the expected by factors of roughly 7–12 across all three mediator hypotheses and three independent background model variants, driven by a residual background-modelling discrepancy in the high-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} tail (ETmiss≥100GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}\ge100\,\text{GeV}, 2.4–2.5% of SR events) rather than evidence of a DM signal. To our knowledge this is the first application of Neural Spline Flow likelihood-ratio scoring to a mono-ZZ DM search on CMS open Run 2015D data in both the μμ\mu\mu and eeee channels simultaneously.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-introduction@@@@TOK:SECNUM:1. @@Introduction

Astrophysical evidence points to a non-baryonic dark component of the Universe, but the microscopic identity of dark matter (DM) remains unknown. Among the experimental approaches, high-energy colliders can produce DM in controlled environments and probe its couplings to Standard Model (SM) particles through missing transverse momentum (ETmiss\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}) recoiling against visible final-state objects@@TOK:CITE:Goodman2011@@. The mono-ZZ topology—a leptonically decaying ZZ boson balanced by ETmiss\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}—offers a relatively clean signature: the dilepton system constrains the ZZ mass, electroweak backgrounds are smaller than in hadronic mono-jet searches, and the channel is sensitive to simplified models with ss-channel mediators that also radiate an on-shell ZZ boson@@TOK:CITE:Bell2012,Carpenter2013,DMForum2015@@.

Published LHC searches in this final state have progressed from early reinterpretations and multivariate projections to full Run 2 results from CMS and ATLAS@@TOK:CITE:Alves2015,CMS2017MonoZ,CMS2021MonoZ@@. These analyses typically bin events in ETmiss\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}} or related kinematic variables, fit parametric or template background models, and compare data to signal hypotheses defined within the ATLAS/CMS Dark Matter Forum benchmark framework@@TOK:CITE:DMForum2015@@. Complementary mono-ZZ and mono-Z′Z' phenomenology has also been studied through dilepton mass spectra, angular distributions, and resonance-plus-ETmiss\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}} topologies@@TOK:CITE:Autran2015,Altmannshofer2015,Yang2017,Elgammal2024@@.

We investigate an alternative strategy that keeps the mono-ZZ event selection but replaces hand-crafted discriminant variables with a likelihood-ratio score built from learned event densities. Neural Spline Flows (NSFs)@@TOK:CITE:Durkan2019,Papamakarios2021@@ model the SM background and three DM mediator hypotheses as continuous distributions over a fixed set of cleaned kinematic features; the per-event score is the log-density difference between the selected DM hypothesis and the channel-specific SM hypothesis. Normalizing flows supply exact density evaluation for correlated inputs@@TOK:CITE:Papamakarios2021,Reyes2022@@, and surjective or simulation-aware extensions have been explored for collider event modeling and anomaly detection@@TOK:CITE:Verheyen2022,Andreassen2020@@. Our implementation uses CMS Run 2015D open data@@TOK:CITE:CERNOpenDataDoubleMuon,CERNOpenDataDoubleEG@@ for SM-dominated control samples and publicly released MonoZToLL MC samples with vector, axial-vector, and scalar mediators@@TOK:CITE:CERNOpenDataMonoZVector,CERNOpenDataMonoZAxialMx10Mv20,CERNOpenDataMonoZAxialMx50Mv200,CERNOpenDataMonoZScalar@@ for signal studies, with one DM flow trained per mediator model and identical physics-feature definitions in the μμ\mu\mu and eeee channels.

Section @@TOK:REF:sec:literature@@ reviews the mono-ZZ literature and related machine-learning methods in more detail. Section @@TOK:REF:sec:objective@@ states the analysis regions and score definition used here; Section @@TOK:REF:sec:outline@@ summarizes our contributions and the paper structure. All region definitions, training splits, and numerical results reported below follow our analysis pipeline unless explicitly attributed to external work.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-objective@@@@TOK:SECNUM:1.1 @@Analysis objective

The physics goal is to test whether the number and kinematics of events in a signal region are consistent with SM production of Z+ETmissZ+\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}, or require a DM contribution. The working signal hypotheses are DM pair production with vector, axial-vector, and scalar mediators, simulated with the same lepton selection as data.

Operationally, we partition events into a control region (CR) and a signal region (SR). The CR (ETmiss<50GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}<50\,\text{GeV}) is dominated by Drell–Yan ZZ+jets and is used only to train SM density models; it is disjoint from the SR by construction. The SR applies ETmiss≥50GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}\ge 50\,\text{GeV}, |Δϕ(ETmiss,Z)|>2.5|\Delta\phi(\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}},Z)|>2.5, and njets≤1n_{\mathrm{jets}}\le 1. The SR imposes no explicit upper ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} requirement; in practice the cleaned dataset caps ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} at approximately 200  (Section @@TOK:REF:sec:datasets@@), so events enter the fit through ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} values up to this bound. The validation interval (50≤ETmiss<100GeV50\le\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}<100\,\text{GeV}) additionally serves as a background-normalisation sideband in the simultaneous SR+VR fit: it constrains the per-channel normalisation nuisances using VR data independently of the SR, providing a genuine sideband-based background prediction.

Two SM flows are trained, one on CR double-muon events and one on CR double-electron events (70%/30% train/validation split in each channel). Three DM flows are trained, one each on the full vector, axial-vector, and scalar MC samples, and are shared across lepton channels. The branch lepton_flavor (1111 for electrons, 1313 for muons) selects the channel but is not included in the NSF input vector.

Before interpreting the SR, we check the SM-only flow in a 50–100  ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} validation band to verify stable channel-specific SM behavior. The primary search constructs six SR score arrays, 𝒮hμμ\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}_h^{\mu\mu} and 𝒮hee\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}_h^{ee} for the three mediator hypotheses, and fits three combined binned μμ\mu\mu+ee likelihoods, one per signal hypothesis@@TOK:CITE:Cowan2011@@.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-literature@@@@TOK:SECNUM:2. @@Literature Review

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-lit-pheno@@@@TOK:SECNUM:2.1 @@Collider constraints and mono-Z phenomenology

Early collider studies showed that effective-field-theory (EFT) descriptions of DM coupling to quarks and gluons can yield limits on interaction scales that complement, and in some kinematic regimes surpass, direct-detection bounds—particularly for light DM and spin-dependent scattering@@TOK:CITE:Goodman2011@@. The mono-ZZ channel was proposed as an electroweak counterpart to mono-jet and mono-photon searches: a ZZ boson recoils against a pair of stable DM particles, with the ZZ emitted from initial-state quarks or an internal line in a simplified model@@TOK:CITE:Bell2012@@. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Carpenter2013@@ extended mono-XX reinterpretations to events with a reconstructed Z→ℓ+ℓ−Z\to\ell^+\ell^- system, deriving limits on EFT operators with quark bilinear and electroweak-boson couplings from ATLAS ZZ+ETmiss\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}} measurements.

Related signatures broaden the mono-ZZ program beyond a single on-shell ZZ recoiling against DM alone. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Autran2015@@ studied ETmiss\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}} in association with a dilepton or dijet resonance from a Z′Z' boson, highlighting sensitivity in mass regions where standard resonance searches lose efficiency. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Altmannshofer2015@@ pointed out that DM with scalar mediators can induce loop-level box corrections to Drell–Yan production, producing a “monocline” feature near mℓℓ∼2mχm_{\ell\ell}\sim 2m_\chi rather than a narrow resonance. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Berlin2015@@ discussed pseudoscalar-portal fermion DM in two-Higgs-doublet setups, where chiral couplings suppress direct detection while preserving thermal relic targets. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Liew2017@@ compared mono-XX searches with direct searches for the mediating particle across several simplified models, finding that mono-XX is most competitive when the spectrum is compressed or when electroweak bosons participate directly in the mediator–DM coupling. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Yang2017@@ showed that lepton angular distributions in the Collins–Soper frame can distinguish spin-0, spin-1, and spin-2 mediator scenarios in mono-ZZ production and that shape information beyond inclusive ETmiss\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}} can strengthen coupling limits.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-lit-experiment@@@@TOK:SECNUM:2.2 @@Simplified models, benchmarks, and experimental searches

The ATLAS/CMS Dark Matter Forum report@@TOK:CITE:DMForum2015@@ codified simplified models —ss-channel vector, axial-vector, scalar, and pseudoscalar mediators, tt-channel colored mediators, and EFT benchmarks—together with recommended parameter scans and implementation guidance for Run 2 ETmiss+X\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}+X analyses. These models underpin the MonoZToLL open-data samples used in our signal studies; vector, axial-vector, and scalar mediator samples are each extracted and assigned a separate DM flow for hypothesis-specific scoring@@TOK:CITE:CERNOpenDataMonoZVector,CERNOpenDataMonoZAxialMx10Mv20,CERNOpenDataMonoZAxialMx50Mv200,CERNOpenDataMonoZScalar,DMForum2015@@.

On the experimental side, CMS published mono-ZZ searches at s=13\sqrt{s}=13 TeV interpreting spin-0 and spin-1 mediators, invisible Higgs decays, and other ETmiss\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}+ℓ+ℓ−\ell^+\ell^- scenarios@@TOK:CITE:CMS2017MonoZ,CMS2021MonoZ@@. The most recent CMS result uses the full Run 2 dataset (137fb−1137~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}) and sets limits on mediator and DM masses for vector, axial-vector, scalar, and pseudoscalar mediators, with comparisons to direct-detection cross sections@@TOK:CITE:CMS2021MonoZ@@. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Alves2015@@ projected the mono-ZZ reach at 1313 TeV with a multivariate likelihood discriminant, reporting roughly a factor-of-two improvement over cut-and-count methods once background normalization systematics exceed a few percent. High-mass dilepton searches from ATLAS@@TOK:CITE:ATLAS2016Dilepton,ATLAS2017Dilepton@@ bound Z′Z' resonances and four-fermion contact interactions; while not mono-ZZ analyses, they constrain overlapping ZZ-associated new-physics scenarios probed in Refs.@@TOK:CITE:Autran2015,Elgammal2024@@. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Elgammal2024@@ applied CMS open data from Run 1 to dimuon angular distributions at high mμμm_{\mu\mu}, interpreting the data in a mono-Z′Z' simplified model and finding agreement with SM Drell–Yan expectations.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-lit-ml@@@@TOK:SECNUM:2.3 @@Density estimation and learning-based search methods

Normalizing flows map a simple base density to a target distribution through invertible transformations, enabling both sampling and exact likelihood evaluation@@TOK:CITE:Papamakarios2021@@. Neural Spline Flows replace elementwise affine couplings with monotonic rational-quadratic splines, increasing expressivity while retaining analytic inversion@@TOK:CITE:Durkan2019@@. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Reyes2022@@ examined how several flow architectures scale with input dimensionality on toy HEP-like datasets, a relevant consideration for multivariate collider features. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Verheyen2022@@ introduced surjective and stochastic flow layers to handle permutation symmetry, variable particle multiplicities, and discrete quantum numbers in matrix-element-level and detector-level event generation, as well as anomaly-detection studies. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Andreassen2020@@ proposed simulation-assisted likelihood-free anomaly detection (SALAD), reweighting background simulation to data in sidebands before supervised learning in a signal-sensitive region—a hybrid of data-driven and simulation-based approaches distinct from our explicit two-hypothesis density ratio. Closely related anomaly-detection methods that use sideband density estimates include ANODE@@TOK:CITE:Nachman2020@@ and CATHODE@@TOK:CITE:Hallin2021@@; both construct signal-region classifiers from control-region density ratios, a strategy adjacent to our direct NSF log-ratio score. Ref.@@TOK:CITE:Alves2015@@ remains the closest prior mono-ZZ reference for multivariate discrimination, though it used engineered kinematic inputs and a likelihood function rather than normalizing-flow densities trained separately on control-region data and signal MC.

Our analysis sits at the intersection of these threads: we adopt the mono-ZZ simplified-model context and CR/SR philosophy of the experimental and Forum literature@@TOK:CITE:DMForum2015,CMS2021MonoZ@@, but estimate SM and DM event densities with NSFs and use their log-ratio as the test statistic, following asymptotic inference prescriptions@@TOK:CITE:Cowan2011@@ in the signal region. To our knowledge, a published mono-ZZ DM search combining NSF-based likelihood ratios with CMS open Run 2015D data in both μμ\mu\mu and eeee channels has not been reported; the present work documents that pipeline through the final SR likelihood fit.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-preprocessing@@@@TOK:SECNUM:4. @@Feature Extraction and Cleaning

Raw MINIAOD and MINIAODSIM files are read directly with uproot, with jagged collections handled in awkward and converted to fixed per-event NumPy arrays after object selection. The analysis products are ROOT Events TTrees written with uproot.recreate, mktree, and extend; CSV files are used only for cut-flow, file-audit, and EDA summary outputs. Per-event quantities include ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}, dilepton and ZZ kinematics, jet and bb-tag summaries, hadronic recoil variables, and angular correlations (40 physics features in the extracted schema). The complete list of stored branches and formulas for derived observables is given in Appendix @@TOK:REF:app:features@@; Table @@TOK:REF:tab:extraction-cuts@@ lists the selection cuts implemented in the extraction notebooks.

The DoubleMuon and DoubleEG extractors use a custom trigger-object decoder because the 2015 MiniAOD trigger information is not exposed as simple one-branch-per-HLT-path booleans. The decoder reads the selectedPatTrigger path-name, pathLastFilterAccepted, and filter-label collections. When path names are available, events are matched to the channel-specific HLT prefixes; when path names are empty, the extractor falls back to accepted final-filter labels such as the double-muon or double-electron filter tokens. Trigger decisions are accumulated in TRIGGER_AUDIT and the retained event mask is applied before physics-feature writing. For the data bb veto, the notebooks also decode the MiniAOD pat::Jet::pairDiscriVector_ payload using uproot’s AsBinary interpretation. A slow decoder discovers the discriminator labels, a cached fixed-layout fast path extracts the CSVv2 scores, and a validation step checks the fast decoder against the slow decoder on sampled events. The medium 2015/76X CSVv2 working point (0.8000.800) is then used to compute n_bjets, max_btag_csvv2, and the bb-veto audit branches.

Cleaning is applied only to SM data TTrees. The SM cleaner clips fixed-domain variables, estimates high-quantile caps for heavy-tailed variables, and uses the opposite SM lepton channel as the reference sample when deriving channel-consistent caps. The DoubleMuon cleaner therefore references DoubleEG parts, and the DoubleEG cleaner references DoubleMuon parts. The merged SM bounds are exported to Output/sm_feature_bounds.json; DM MC ROOT files are not cleaned by cleaner.py. Instead, the exported SM bounds are applied later at DataLoader time, so genuine high-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} or high-pTp_{\mathrm{T}} DM tails are preserved in the extracted MC files until the training pipeline applies the common SM feature domain. The fixed bounds and tail-rule families used by the cleaning script are summarized in Appendix @@TOK:REF:app:cleaning@@.

The cleaned NSF feature manifest retains 35 extracted physics features and excludes lepton_flavor, n_bjets, gen_weight, rho, n_vertices, and raw HT; lepton_flavor is retained only for channel routing and is not passed to any flow. Two binary jet-presence indicators computed at DataLoader time (has_jet1=𝟙[njets≥1]=\mathbb{1}[n_\mathrm{jets}\ge 1], has_jet2=𝟙[njets≥2]=\mathbb{1}[n_\mathrm{jets}\ge 2]) are appended to form the final 37-dimensional NSF input.

The five NSFs share the following hyperparameters: 8 masked autoregressive coupling transforms with rational-quadratic splines (8 bins per spline, randomised feature permutation per transform), conditioner networks with two hidden layers of 256 units each, and a standard-normal base distribution. Training uses the Adam optimiser (η=3×10−4\eta=3\times10^{-4}, λ=10−5\lambda=10^{-5} weight decay) with a 500-step linear warm-up followed by cosine annealing to ηmin=10−6\eta_\mathrm{min}=10^{-6} over 200 epochs, a mini-batch size of 2048, and gradient clipping at ∥∇∥2≤1\|\nabla\|_2\le 1. Early stopping monitors validation NLL with a patience of 20 epochs and restores the best-epoch weights. In the present training runs, validation NLL continued to decrease at epoch 200 for all five flows; early stopping was not triggered for any flow before reaching MAX_EPOCHS. Every _best.pt checkpoint therefore corresponds to the final epoch rather than a converged minimum, and we flag potential residual undertraining as a limitation affecting the learned densities and reported limits. The discrete feature n_jets is dequantized during training by adding uniform noise 𝒰(−0.5,0.5)\mathcal{U}(-0.5,0.5); raw integer values are used at inference.

All five flows operate in the same standardized feature space. SM flows fit a per-feature zz-score scaler on their respective training splits and save the parameters to a JSON manifest. DM flows reuse the NSF_SM_mumu scaler rather than fitting new scale parameters, ensuring that DM event densities are evaluated in the SM feature domain. Before standardization, DM MC features are clipped to the SM bounds exported in Output/sm_feature_bounds.json; this preserves genuine high-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} tails while preventing out-of-domain extrapolation of the learned SM density.

The total training campaign therefore consists of five independent NSF runs: two channel-specific SM flows and three mediator-specific DM flows. Closure checks use the 50–100  validation band to verify the channel-specific SM models before SR score arrays are interpreted. The axial-vector μμ\mu\mu score distribution shows a localised bin-wobble pattern near 𝒮h≈0\mathcal{S}_h\approx0–50, consistent with residual NSF spline instability from non-convergence; this feature appears in all three fit variants and is therefore a property of the trained flow rather than an artifact of any single background model.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-search@@@@TOK:SECNUM:7. @@Likelihood-Ratio Search

The search uses 𝒮h\ensuremath{\mathcal{S}}_h as the per-event test statistic for each mediator hypothesis (Eq. @@TOK:EQREF:eq:score@@). For μμ\mu\mu SR events, vector, axial-vector, and scalar DM log densities are compared with NSF_SM_mumu; for eeee SR events the same three DM log densities are compared with NSF_SM_ee. This yields six score arrays in total. We perform three simultaneous SR+VR binned profile-likelihood fits for signal strength μ\mu, one for each DM hypothesis. Each fit combines the μμ\mu\mu and eeee score histograms from both the SR and VR simultaneously: ln⁡ℒ=ln⁡ℒSR+ln⁡ℒVR−12θμμ2−12θee2,\ln\mathcal{L} = \ln\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SR}} + \ln\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VR}} - \tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{\mu\mu}^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{ee}^2,@@TOK:EQA:eq-anon-2@@ where ℒSR\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SR}} and ℒVR\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VR}} are binned Poisson likelihoods over the score histograms in each region, the signal strength μ\mu and the per-channel normalisation nuisances θμμ\theta_{\mu\mu}, θee\theta_{ee} are shared between regions, and the background templates in both regions are derived from a single-step VR→\toSR shape transfer: the SM VR score histogram is renormalised to the respective region yield and used as the nominal background prediction for that region. The observed data histograms are the raw SR and VR score counts, which differ from the renormalised VR background template by real Poisson fluctuations and by genuine shape differences between the VR and SR score distributions. The VR component constrains the background normalisation using ∼\sim6,970 (μμ\mu\mu) and ∼\sim6,644 (eeee) VR events independently of the SR, providing a genuine sideband-based background prediction. Signal MC contributes to both SR and VR components with the same μ\mu. Asymptotic formulae@@TOK:CITE:Cowan2011@@ are used for the discovery test statistic and CLs\mathrm{CL}_{s} upper limits.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-systematics@@@@TOK:SECNUM:7.1 @@Systematic uncertainties

The profile-likelihood fit includes independent Gaussian-constrained normalisation nuisance parameters for the μμ\mu\mu and eeee channels with a prior width of 5% (σ=0.05\sigma=0.05). In the simultaneous SR+VR fit the normalisation nuisances are constrained additionally by the VR data; the posterior uncertainty on θnorm\theta_{\mathrm{norm}} is approximately 0.17 (compared to the prior of 1.0 in sigma units), corresponding to an effective normalisation constraint of ∼\sim0.85% from the VR statistics alone.

Integrated luminosity (propagated).

The Run 2015D dataset corresponds to ℒ=2.32±0.037fb−1\mathcal{L}=2.32\pm0.037\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1} (uncertainty 1.6%), taken from the CMS precision luminosity measurement@@TOK:CITE:CMS-LUM-2021@@. A correlated log-normal luminosity nuisance is applied across both channels.

Lepton efficiency (propagated).

Combined identification, reconstruction, and trigger efficiency uncertainties of approximately 3% per channel are applied as independent per-channel log-normal nuisances, following the treatment in the CMS Run 2 mono-ZZ search at the same centre-of-mass energy@@TOK:CITE:CMS2017MonoZ@@. Lepton momentum-scale uncertainties of 1% (μ\mu) and 2%/5% (electrons, barrel/endcap) are included as additional shape nuisances on the lepton pTp_{\mathrm{T}} distributions.

MET resolution and pileup (not propagated).

Evaluating ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} resolution and pileup-reweighting uncertainties requires re-scoring SR, VR, and CR events with shifted inputs, which constitutes new analysis work not performed in this study. These sources are acknowledged as a gap in the systematic budget.

Combined systematic budget.

Treating luminosity as fully correlated across channels and lepton efficiency as per-channel independent, the combined propagated external systematic is approximately 1.62+32+32≈4.5%\sqrt{1.6^2+3^2+3^2}\approx4.5\% per channel, on top of the 0.85% data-driven normalisation constraint. The dominant uncertainty is the unresolved high-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} tail modelling residual (Section @@TOK:REF:sec:met-tail@@).

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-met-tail@@@@TOK:SECNUM:7.2 @@High-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} tail modelling residual

Events with ETmiss≥100GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}\ge100\,\text{GeV} constitute 2.4–2.5% of the SR (∼\sim160–181 events across both channels) but carry mean NSF scores 140–195 units higher than the VR bulk — a shift exceeding the score distribution’s own standard deviation of 40–60 units. The single-step VR background has negligible support at these scores, so the tail population appears as a systematic upward fluctuation in the fit.

To characterise this effect, we tested linear and quadratic ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}-dependent models of the CR/VR score trend, validating each against held-out VR bins before extrapolating to the SR tail (Section @@TOK:REF:sec:extrapolation-validation@@). Neither functional form reliably predicts the observed tail behaviour: the linear model underpredicts the true SR-tail mean score by 90–115 units, while the quadratic model overpredicts by 300–330 units. The limited tail statistics (160–181 events across 100 ) do not support more flexible extrapolation.

The VR-shape background is therefore used as nominal, and the resulting inflation of μ̂\hat\mu, q0q_0, and the observed-to-expected limit ratios reflects this unresolved modelling residual rather than evidence of DM production.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-extrapolation-validation@@@@TOK:SECNUM:7.3 @@NSF CR→\toSR extrapolation validation

To validate that the NSF SM density, trained only on the control region (ETmiss<50GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}<50\,\text{GeV}), extrapolates in a characterised way into the signal region, we measured the mean NSF score as a function of ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} across CR and VR bins for all three mediator hypotheses and both lepton channels (six combinations), then tested two extrapolation models against held-out data.

Procedure.

Events in the CR and VR were binned by ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} in 10 GeV intervals. For each bin the mean NSF score ⟨𝒮h⟩\langle\mathcal{S}_h\rangle was computed. The two highest-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} VR bins (80–100 GeV) were held out as a validation set. Linear and quadratic functions of ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} were fit to the remaining bins and extrapolated to the SR tail (ETmiss≥100GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}\ge100\,\text{GeV}), where the true mean score was measured independently.

Results.

The linear fit undershot the held-out VR bins by 0.3–0.6σ\sigma (systematic across all six combinations). When extrapolated to ETmiss∼150GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}\sim150\,\text{GeV} it overshot the true SR-tail mean score by 90–115 units. The quadratic fit overshot the held-out bins by 1.1–3.3σ\sigma (in the opposite direction from the linear case) and overshot the true SR-tail mean by 300–330 units; the positive curvature in all six fits indicates the quadratic form was dominated by the steep low-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} rise rather than the true behaviour at higher ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}.

Conclusion.

Neither functional form reliably extrapolates the CR-trained NSF density into the high-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} SR tail. Combined with the limited tail statistics (160–181 events across 100 ), this demonstrates that the NSF’s CR-trained density does not extrapolate smoothly or predictably into the sparse high-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} signal region. This validates and motivates the treatment of the resulting background modelling residual as an explicit, quantified limitation (Section @@TOK:REF:sec:met-tail@@) rather than an uncorrected prediction.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-results@@@@TOK:SECNUM:8. @@Results

The normalizing-flow likelihood-ratio discriminant was evaluated using disjoint control-region (CR), validation-region (VR), and signal-region (SR) samples. The Standard Model density flows were trained only on CR events with ETmiss<50GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}<50\,\text{GeV}. The final statistical interpretation uses the full SR selection, ETmiss≥50GeV\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}\ge50\,\text{GeV}, |Δϕ(ETmiss,Z)|>2.5|\Delta\phi(\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}},Z)|>2.5, and njets≤1n_{\mathrm{jets}}\le1. Although no explicit upper ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} bound is imposed in the selection, the cleaned dataset caps ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} at approximately 200 , and the VR interval (50≤ETmiss<100GeV50\le\ensuremath{E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}}<100\,\text{GeV}) is retained as a validation and stress-test sample rather than as the nominal background template for the final limits.

Control-region closure tests show stable channel-specific behaviour of the trained SM flows. The CR distributions of log⁡p(𝐱∣SM)\log p(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathrm{SM}) peak near ∼125\sim 125 with similar widths in the μμ\mu\mu and eeee channels, consistent with a well-converged density model on Drell–Yan-dominated events.

Validation-region score distributions show clear separation between observed data and simulated dark matter events for all mediator hypotheses. The strongest discrimination is observed for the scalar mediator, whose signal distribution occupies a largely distinct region of score space. Consistent behaviour is observed in both lepton channels, demonstrating that the NSF score generalises across detector signatures and event topologies.

In all cases, the observed data are concentrated at lower score values, whereas simulated dark matter events populate the high-score tail. The scalar hypothesis produces the largest signal-background separation, followed by the vector and axial-vector models.

A binned profile-likelihood fit was performed using the NSF score distributions. Initial histograms were constructed using 30 score bins and subsequently merged to ensure a minimum nominal background yield of 20 events per bin. The nominal background shapes are derived from a single-step VR→\toSR transfer, while the final statistical interpretation uses a simultaneous SR+VR profile-likelihood fit with independent 5% normalization nuisance parameters in the μμ\mu\mu and eeee channels. The resulting limits are summarized in Table @@TOK:REF:tab:fit_summary@@, while the corresponding confidence-level scans are shown in Fig. @@TOK:REF:fig:cls_scans@@. The vector and axial-vector hypotheses yield observed limits of μ<0.0362\mu < 0.0362 and μ<0.0498\mu < 0.0498, respectively.

For all three mediator hypotheses the observed limits exceed the median expected limits by factors of roughly 7–12 across the nominal and cross-check background constructions, reflecting the unresolved high-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} tail modelling residual described in Section @@TOK:REF:sec:met-tail@@. The superior performance of the scalar model is consistent with its significantly stronger separation in NSF score space. The axial-vector hypothesis exhibits the weakest sensitivity owing to the greater overlap between signal and background score distributions.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-sec-summary@@@@TOK:SECNUM:9. @@Summary and Outlook

We presented a mono-ZZ DM search that replaces hand-crafted cut flows with likelihood-ratio scores from Neural Spline Flows trained on CR data and DM MC. The workflow—open-data extraction, audited cleaning, disjoint CR/SR design, and three combined μμ\mu\mu+ee fits from five trained flows—provides a reproducible template for density-based searches at the LHC. The final simultaneous SR+VR fits yield observed 95% CL limits of μ<0.0177\mu<0.0177 (scalar), μ<0.0362\mu<0.0362 (vector), and μ<0.0498\mu<0.0498 (axial-vector), corresponding to the cross-section limits reported in Table @@TOK:REF:tab:fit_summary@@. The observed-to-expected gaps are driven by the high-ETmissE_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}} tail modelling residual rather than by a dark matter signal. A pure VR-extrapolated shape alternative fails closure in the high-score tail (Appendix @@TOK:REF:app:vr-extrapolation@@) and is not used for the final limits.

@@TOK:HEADID:paper-app-background-method@@@@TOK:SECNUM:D.3 @@Background estimation method note

The simultaneous SR+VR fit uses a single-step VR→\toSR shape transfer: the SM VR score histogram is renormalised to the region yield and used as the nominal background template for that region. The VR component of the likelihood constrains the per-channel normalisation nuisances θnorm\theta_{\mathrm{norm}} using ∼\sim6,970 (μμ\mu\mu) and ∼\sim6,644 (eeee) VR events; the posterior uncertainty on θnorm\theta_{\mathrm{norm}} is ∼\sim0.17 in sigma units, significantly tighter than the 5% Gaussian prior alone. Signal MC contributes to both SR and VR with the same signal strength μ\mu, so the VR signal contamination is accounted for. This approach avoids a pure SR-direct circularity while keeping the SR score shape as the primary discriminant. Bins with zero nominal background are excluded from the likelihood.
