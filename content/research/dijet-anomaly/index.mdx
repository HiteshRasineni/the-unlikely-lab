## Overview

This study applies unsupervised density estimation to CMS dijet events. A Neural Spline Flow is trained on the distribution of dijet event observables; each event is then assigned a likelihood-based anomaly score proportional to how unlikely it is under the learned density. The approach requires no labels and no reference signal model.

## Physics Motivation

Dijet events â€” two high-transverse-momentum hadronic jets recoiling against each other â€” are among the most abundant signatures in protonâ€“proton collisions, which makes them a natural and statistically powerful place to search for new phenomena that would appear as local excesses of unusual event structure. New particles decaying to quarks or gluons can manifest as resonances or as broad distortions of the jet kinematic distributions.

Rather than specifying a particular signal hypothesis, this study asks a more general question: which observed events look least like the bulk of the data? Density estimation provides the formal answer through the likelihood.

## Neural Spline Flow

The density model for this study uses the same Neural Spline Flow architecture family as the mono-Z analysis: a stack of identical transformation blocks built from masked piecewise rational quadratic autoregressive transforms with random permutations between blocks. The precise hyperparameters recorded for this study's training runs will be documented here once consolidated from the training configuration files. No unverified values are listed.

## Anomaly Score

Given a trained flow model, the log-likelihood $\log p(\mathbf{x})$ of each event is evaluated exactly, using the invertibility of the transformation and the tractable Jacobian determinant. Events with low probability under the learned density receive the highest anomaly scores:

$$
s(\mathbf{x}) = -\log p(\mathbf{x})
$$

Low-scoring events concentrate in the tails of the learned distribution, where rare or unexpected structure lives â€” but also where detector effects and mismodeling can live. See [Why Do Anomalies Live in the Tails?](/notes/anomaly-tails).
