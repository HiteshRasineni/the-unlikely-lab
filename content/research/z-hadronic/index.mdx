## Overview

This study presents a projected sensitivity study for hadronic mono-$Z$ dark-matter production ($pp \to Z\bar{\chi}\chi$ with $Z \to jj$) using CMS Run 2015D HTMHT open data corresponding to $2.256382381~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$, from which 1,439,523 events satisfy the hadronic mono-$Z$ selection.

Backgrounds are modelled with a conditional flow-matching continuous normalizing flow (CFM-CNF) trained on the selected HTMHT events and evaluated on a held-out validation split reweighted to the full selected population.

## Physics Motivation

Collider experiments offer a complementary route to direct and indirect dark matter detection. In the mono-$Z$ topology, a $Z$ boson is produced in association with a pair of stable DM particles escaping undetected as missing transverse momentum ($\MET$):

- The hadronic decay channel $Z \rightarrow q\bar{q}$ benefits from a substantially larger branching fraction ($\sim 70\%$) compared to leptonic decay channels.
- Multijet background modeling in this channel is challenging, making continuous-time density estimation particularly advantageous.
- Extra-jet topology carries substantial discriminating power between mono-$Z$ dark-matter production and generic multijet QCD/electroweak backgrounds.

## Dataset

Events are drawn from the CMS Run 2015D HTMHT MINIAOD open dataset ($2.256382381~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$). Selected events satisfy at least two jets with $p_T > 30~\mathrm{GeV}$ and $|\eta| < 2.4$, dijet mass $70 < m_{jj} < 110~\mathrm{GeV}$, $\Delta R_{jj} < 2.0$, and a $b$-tag veto on extra jets.

Simulated dark matter signal samples are generated with MadGraph5 + Pythia8 + Delphes using the `DMsimp_s_spin1` simplified model for axial-vector and vector mediators.

## Density Estimation & Flow Matching

The background density is modelled with a continuous normalizing flow parameterized by a learned velocity field $v_\theta(\mathbf{z}(t), t)$ trained with the simulation-free conditional flow matching objective:

```text
Target Data Point (x₁) ──┐
                         ├──► Velocity Path Regression ──► Probability-Flow ODE
Base Gaussian (x₀)     ──┘
```

Per-event negative log-likelihoods (NLL) obtained via Hutchinson trace estimation along the probability-flow ODE serve as the one-sided discriminant.

## Key Results

Under the baseline analysis, the projected discovery sensitivities evaluated with the Asimov formula ($B \geq 20$ floor) are:

- **Axial benchmark** ($m_\chi=10~\mathrm{GeV}$, $M_{\mathrm{med}}=20~\mathrm{GeV}$): $2.89\sigma$ expected significance.
- **Axial benchmark** ($m_\chi=50~\mathrm{GeV}$, $M_{\mathrm{med}}=200~\mathrm{GeV}$): $7.62\sigma$ expected significance.
- **Vector benchmark** ($m_\chi=1~\mathrm{GeV}$, $M_{\mathrm{med}}=500~\mathrm{GeV}$): $7.41\sigma$ expected significance.

An ablation study removing extra-jet kinematic features reduces expected significance by 53–71%, confirming the critical role of extra-jet topology in the hadronic channel.
